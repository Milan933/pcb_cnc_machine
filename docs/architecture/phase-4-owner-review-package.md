## 3. Structural joint count and risk

Two counts are needed because the CAD validator and a physical assembly count
different things:

- The automated model contains **34 expected solid-overlap pair events**. This
  is the auditable interference graph used by `phase4_assembly.py`; several
  entries are two-sided contacts around one removable module.
- The physical owner-review register contains **28 planned PETG-to-PETG joint
  groups**, counting a mirrored/two-host cartridge or foot as one physical
  module interface. Butt seams and service shoulders that do not overlap as
  solids are included in this register. Final fastener counts remain open.

The 28 planned groups are: four base-to-side seams; two Y-carrier/base module
interfaces; one center-tie/carrier interface; one center-tie/side closure; one
Y-motor/front-base module; one Y-fixed/front-base module; one Y-floating/rear-
base module; four foot/base modules; two tower/base shoulders; one Y motor to
fixed-cartridge service stack; two X bearing cartridge/beam interfaces; two
beam/tower joints; one J1 beam split; one X/Z guide/backplate module interface;
one Z fixed-support/XZ interface; one Z motor/support interface; one
spindle-mount/Z-carriage interface; and one optional electronics-rail/base
interface.

These are not production joint specifications. The default interface policy is
M4 heat-set inserts for general structural, motor, bearing, spindle, and module
joints; M3 for rail/accessory hardware; and M5 only after a documented load
justification. Fasteners provide preload. Shoulders, pockets, registrations,
keys, tongues, and mating faces must carry location and shear.

The approximate local counts above should not be summed into a purchase order.
The current full structural concept is expected to use roughly **80-100 M4
structural/module screw or insert positions**, plus supplier-dependent M3 rail
and accessory hardware. Through-bolts remain a selective alternative if insert
pull-out, creep, or joint moment evidence requires them.

Highest compliance/creep risks are J1, the 300 mm Y carrier datum interfaces,
tower/base shoulders, the X/Z rail/backplate interface, and the moving-bed
carriage pads. A loss of preload or datum at any of these can cause rail
binding, gantry slip, bed pitch error, or tool-point drift even if the bulk
PETG sections remain intact.

## 4. Primary force-loop trace

The intended force loop is:

```text
tool
  -> spindle / clamp (reference metal + spindle_mount_concept PETG)
  -> z_carriage_plate PETG
  -> MGN9 Z guides and x_carriage_plate PETG
  -> MGN12 X guides and gantry_beam_left/right PETG
  -> J1 beam split and beam-to-tower shoulders
  -> gantry_tower_left/right PETG
  -> tower/base shoulders and base_y_rail_carrier_left/right PETG
  -> base_center_tie, base side members, and front/rear base segments PETG
  -> MGN12 Y rails and carriages
  -> moving_bed_frame PETG
  -> separate bed support / spoilboard
  -> PCB datum
  -> tool
```

The **primary force-loop PETG part count is 17**. It is 16 CRITICAL
STRUCTURAL parts plus `spindle_mount_concept`, which is classified as a
replaceable interface even though it is directly in the loop. The loop has
**17 pair-level PETG joint interfaces**, or **14 physical groups** after
collapsing two-sided carrier contacts and the mirrored tower/beam interfaces.

Primary PETG parts are the four base segments, two side members, two Y rail
carriers, center tie, two towers, two beam halves, X carriage plate, Z
carriage plate, spindle mount, and moving bed. Secondary cartridges and feet
are identified in the inventory but are not counted as primary force-loop PETG.

The metal interfaces are intentional: X/Y/Z rails and carriages, screws,
bearings, couplers, spindle, bed support, and spoilboard are replaceable. The
current 5 N model does not resolve each interface separately; it uses the
explicit J1 term, an X/Z equivalent, base/tower equivalents, rail-seat
allowance, and moving-bed allowance.

## 7. Gantry review

### Printed gantry inventory

The printed fixed gantry itself is **four parts**: two 54 x 90 x 62 mm hollow
towers and two split X beam segments, 180 x 122 x 90 mm and 172 x 122 x 90 mm.
The two X bearing cartridges are adjacent replaceable service modules, not
counted in the four-part gantry. The X rail seats are integrated as broad pads
in the beam segments; the rail faces still require measurement, shimming, or
skimming.

### J1/J2/J3 and beam-to-tower joints

There are two beam-to-tower joints, one at each tower. The preliminary screen is
about four M4 structural fasteners per joint, with broad seated shoulders doing
the shear and moment transfer; the screw count and insert geometry are not
frozen. The center beam split is one J1 joint, with a deep tongue/socket,
captured shoulder, and broad mating faces. About four M4 inserts/screws are
screened for clamp preload, not for carrying shear by friction alone.

J1 is preferred provisionally because it gives the clearest geometry-first load
path: the tongue and socket carry axial shear, the shoulders carry bending,
and the broad interface resists torsional slip. J2 remains the simpler fallback
if J1 bridge cleanup or insertion is poor. J3 remains a distributed-key
alternative but adds interfaces, insert locations, and assembly sensitivity.
None is production-ready.

### Orientation and deformation

The towers print upright on their 54 x 90 mm bases. The beam halves print flat
or side-supported with their long axes along X and with the rail pads and J1
features inspected after conditioning. The current deflection screen assigns
0.000480 mm to beam bending, 0.000664 mm to beam torsion, 0.000199 mm to tower
bending, and 0.002778 mm to the equivalent gantry joint. The dominant modeled
gantry uncertainty is therefore the joint/interface stiffness, not the ideal
closed-section beam formula.

## 8. Base review

Tower reactions enter the base through broad tower shoulders, Y carrier load
spreading, the two closed side members, and the front/rear perimeter members.
The base is a closed member loop rather than a full printed plate:

```text
front segments + rear segments
       \       /
     side members
          + center tie
          + two Y rail carriers
```

The base carries vertical tower reaction, beam roll/torsion, Y rail pitch/yaw,
fixed/floating screw reactions, and table support through the four feet. The
center tie closes the transverse load path while leaving a centered Y nut/screw
service relief.

The joints whose creep can lose Y rail alignment are, in descending concern:

1. the two long Y carrier-to-base assemblies and their printed/shimmed rail
   datums;
2. the carrier end ribs and center-tie interfaces;
3. the tower/base and tower/carrier shoulders, which can change frame
   squareness and rail parallelism;
4. front/rear base seams around the Y motor and fixed/floating cartridges;
5. foot/base interfaces, which can change table contact and global leveling.

The current `rail-seat/interface allowance` of 0.002 mm is deliberately an
assumption for this entire class of effects. It is not evidence that raw PETG
will hold a rail datum.

## 9. Moving-bed review

The printed moving-bed structural count is **one** part, `moving_bed_frame`,
with a current CAD solid-equivalent mass of **0.354 kg**. Its geometry is a
230 x 180 mm perimeter/cross-rib frame with a centered 40 x 32 x 30 mm Y-nut
boss and four narrow carriage pads aligned with the 220 mm Y guide spacing.
The 8 mm bed support, 12 mm spoilboard, PCB, workholding, carriages, screw,
nut, and cable/probe are separate reference/process items.

The current bed contribution in the 5 N screen is **0.00125 mm** at the PCB
datum, from an equivalent 4,000 N/mm moving-bed stiffness. This is a bed/frame
screen, not a standalone plate-bending solution. A future bed check must use
the measured support material, rib geometry, 220 mm guide span, 5 N worst-case
tool position, workholding, and PCB contact condition.

For comparison with the Phase 2A nominal **1.24 kg** moving mass:

| Moving item | Phase 2A nominal / current review assumption |
| --- | ---: |
| PCB | 0.09 kg allowance |
| Spoilboard | 0.30 kg allowance |
| Registration/workholding | 0.10 kg allowance |
| Y carriages, screw, nut, and moving hardware | 0.28 kg allowance |
| Cable/probe | 0.05 kg allowance |
| Current printed frame, conceptual installed mass | 0.18-0.25 kg |
| **Current moving assembly excluding a separate support plate** | **1.00-1.07 kg** |
| Separate 230 x 180 x 8 mm support plate, material-dependent | **not frozen** |
| **Current moving assembly if a separate 0.20-0.42 kg support is retained** | **1.20-1.49 kg** |

The frame must not be counted together with the old 0.42 kg “printed moving-bed
support” allowance unless the design truly retains both as physical parts. The
complete-mass result is therefore not yet accepted; it depends on whether the
8 mm support is a light replaceable plate or another dense printed part. The
centered T8x4 nut boss is serviceable in concept, but the actual anti-backlash
nut, retention, preload, and cable path remain open. Tape and low-profile
edge-clamp workholding remain the current process concept.

## 13. Conclusion: answers A-J and recommendation

| Answer | Owner-review conclusion |
| --- | --- |
| **A - What is printed?** | 29 named PETG parts, of which 16 are classified as critical structural. The decomposition is coherent and within the current print boundary, but all records remain preliminary. |
| **B - Is 5.81 kg real?** | It is a CAD solid-equivalent mass, not a measured or slicer mass. A transparent installed-PETG planning range is 3.20-4.36 kg before hardware and bed-process items. |
| **C - Are joints visible and countable?** | Yes. The validator has 34 expected overlap pair events; the physical review register has 28 planned PETG joint groups. J1 and the rail/base interfaces remain the highest evidence risks. |
| **D - Is the force loop complete?** | Yes at concept level: tool, spindle mount, Z/X plates, X beam, towers, base/Y carriers, Y guides, bed, spoilboard, and PCB are all traceable. Metal guide interfaces and physical joint stiffness are still open. |
| **E - Does the deflection screen pass?** | Yes: 0.012648 mm at 5 N passes the 0.020 mm target and 0.030 mm acceptance screen. The result is assumption-dominated and not FEA or measured evidence. |
| **F - Can it be simplified?** | Yes. Every part has a candidate integration, elimination, local-solid reduction, or standardized-metal-interface path. No candidate has been implemented in this package. |
| **G - Is the gantry credible?** | Technically promising. Four printed gantry parts, broad shoulders, integrated rail pads, and J1 make a readable load path; J1 coupons, creep cycling, and repeat-service evidence remain mandatory. |
| **H - Is the base credible?** | The closed member loop is preferable to a full printed slab. Y rail carrier datum creep is the dominant base/alignment risk and needs measurement and coupon evidence. |
| **I - Is the bed mass acceptable?** | The frame alone is lighter than the Phase 2A support allowance, but the complete moving mass is 1.20-1.49 kg if a separate 0.20-0.42 kg support plate remains. This is not yet accepted against the 1.24 kg reference. |
| **J - Is the package ready for Phase 5?** | No. Owner review, actual hardware measurements, PETG/joint/rail-seat coupons, service mock-up, and physical force-loop evidence remain open. |

### Recommendation

**Phase 4A optimization is now accepted as the O2 preliminary structural
architecture baseline.** The remaining actions are hardware procurement,
measurement, coupons, and physical force-loop validation; they do not reopen
mass or part-count optimization. This historical package does not authorize
hardware-specific production CAD or Phase 5.
